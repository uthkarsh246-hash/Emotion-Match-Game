🧠 How does the AI work?
  • Step 1 – Face detection: TinyFaceDetector (a small CNN, SSD-MobileNet style) scans each video frame and returns a box around the face.
  • Step 2 – Expression classification: the face crop goes into FaceExpressionNet, a neural network trained on labelled face photos. It outputs a probability for 7 emotions that add up to 100%.
  • Step 3 – Game logic: we smooth those numbers over a few frames, pick the strongest one, and check if it equals the target emoji for 1 continuous second.
  • Everything runs in your browser through TensorFlow.js (WebGL). The bars in the game are the model's raw confidence – this is the AI "thinking" live.
⚖️ Ethics & Limitations
  • It reads expressions, not feelings. A smile does not prove someone is happy. The model only classifies facial muscle patterns – people mask, fake or feel emotions without showing them.
  • Bias across faces: training datasets over-represent some ages, skin tones, genders and cultures. Accuracy can be lower for under-represented groups, and expression norms differ between cultures.
  • Lighting & camera: dim or back-lit rooms, shadows, low-resolution webcams, glasses, masks, beards and head angles all reduce accuracy.
  • Hard-to-tell emotions: "neutral", "sad" and "angry" are often confused; subtle or mixed expressions are unreliable. Exaggerate for best results.
  • Privacy: frames are processed in memory and discarded. This game never stores or sends images. Real products that analyse emotions (hiring, schools, ads) raise serious consent and surveillance concerns and should not be used for high-stakes decisions.
  • Takeaway: great for a fun game, not a reliable judge of a person's inner state.
